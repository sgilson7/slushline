| 1 | finding | done | **What a frozen-drink machine sounds like, from what could be read.** A frozen carbonated drink is held under pressure (about 37 psi in one patent) and dispensed through a valve to the air, where dissolved gas comes out and it foams to up to double its volume ("overrun"); opening the valve can release "a sudden blast". Sound libraries describe the parts of a fizzy pour as "a soft airy psst of pressure releasing then a fine settling fizz", "a bubbling gurgle with a bright crackling foam rising over it", and a fade to "a faint whisper of bubbles". There is a "Sound Effect - Slushie Machine" video on YouTube (`w8-32wSPy6k`), which could not be listened to from here. The sound's shape follows from these: a psst on opening, a breathy hiss and a low thick body while open, sparse crackle, a soft fade. Sources: US patents 5813574 and 5706661 (USPTO), morphic.com "Soda fizz sounds", Wikipedia "Slurpee". Nothing was downloaded or sampled. |
