# SECOND-ORDER-M2 and M3

M2 (the belt, the lid and a score) and M3 (flavors, orders and blends) were built together and deployed at one gate, because the simulation already carried every order once the belt existed (row 1). Numbers come from `cargo run --release -p lab -- recon-m2` (`analysis/recon-m2.md`), `lab play` (the timer pilot on every mission) and `lab splash`, unless a row names another command.

| 2 | divergence | done | **Slush splashed out of the cups.** A standing cup poured for 20 ticks kept 32 of 44 units; the waste landed just outside both walls (`lab splash`). `A3`: a fast unit driven into the pile is pushed out by the contact passes, and in a Verlet solver that push becomes speed, so it bounced over the wall. `A5`: the textbook explosion of position-based contact, an axiom reversion to water that splashes. Fix: `slush::inelastic` holds a unit's speed along the push it received to what it had that way before the passes, and to nothing if it was moving into the push. A standing cup now keeps 44 of 44 and 83 of 84. `SIM_VERSION` 2. |
| 3 | divergence | done | **With inelastic contacts the swell was hidden again** (`a_cup_keeps_rising_after_the_last_unit_lands`: 78.16 to 78.01 cm). Three fixes were tried and measured. A bounce allowance of 0.3 cm/tick brought the splash back (8 of 84 lost) and stopped the default tuning from resting. Mass scaling by height flattened the slope (0.24) and raised the pilot's waste to 25 to 33 %. Kept: the radius grows with the square of the unit's age, so most of the swell happens after landing. The test passes with six passes. |
